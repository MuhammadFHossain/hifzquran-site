What you need

  • An iPhone or iPad running iOS 17 or later.
  • No permission to start. Location, alerts and alarms are your choice, and the app asks only when you turn one on.
  • An internet connection the first time you play each recitation. After that it is saved on your phone or iPad and plays with no internet.
  • Space. The app is large, over 300 MB once installed, so install it over Wi-Fi.

Common questions

Is there an Android version?

Coming soon. It will be on the Play Store as the same app: the same mushaf, the same repeat, the same prayer times. It will be free, like the app for iPhone and iPad.

How do I repeat an ayah?

Open a surah and tap an ayah on the page. To play more than one, tap the first and the last. The bar at the bottom then says what you have chosen: tap Repeat and it starts playing. The sliders button beside it is where you change how many times each ayah plays, how long a pause to leave so you can recite, and which reciter. With nothing chosen, the same bar shows Listen, which plays from the page you are on to the end of the surah.

How do I get prayer times for my city?

The Salah tab asks where you are praying. Choose a city by name from the built-in list, which needs no permission, or tap Use my location and allow location once. The times, the Hijri date and the qibla direction are then worked out on your phone or iPad.

My mosque's times are different

The app works out the times from your city and a method. Mosques use different methods, and some add a few minutes. Open Settings in the app and tap Salah times. Under Method, choose the one your mosque uses. Under Adjust times, you can move a prayer by a few minutes. If the times still differ, follow your mosque.

The widget shows the wrong times

The widgets read the city from the app. Until you choose one, a widget shows Hifz Quran where the city's name goes, and its times may not be right for you. Open Hifz Quran, go to the Salah tab and choose a city. The widgets follow it after that.

The adhan or the alert did not sound

Each prayer has its own switch for an alert or an adhan in the app. Turn it on, and allow notifications when asked. If your phone is on silent or in a Focus, a normal notification stays quiet. For a prayer that must wake you, turn on Wake me for that prayer. It rings like an alarm, even on silent or in a Focus, and it asks for its own permission. Wake me needs iOS 26.1 or later.

Do I need the internet?

Only for recordings. The mushaf, the Quran text, the translations, the word meanings, the tafsir and the duas are inside the app and work with no internet from the start. The internet is used to fetch a recitation, or a dua read aloud, the first time you play it. After that it is saved on your phone or iPad. You can also save a surah ahead of time from the repeat settings.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Hifz Quran is free forever, and it will never show an ad. There is no subscription, nothing to buy, and no account to make.

Which reciters are included?

31. Every one is recorded ayah by ayah, so a repeat always starts and stops on the ayah. Among them: Mishary Alafasy, Mahmoud Al-Husary, Mohamed El-Minshawi, Abdul Basit, Abdul Rahman As-Sudais, Saud Ash-Shuraim, Saad Al-Ghamdi, Maher Al-Muaiqly and Yasser Ad-Dosari. The full list is in the app.

Where do I find the tafsir?

In the ayah view, tap Tafsir under an ayah. On the mushaf page, tap the ayah, then tap its name in the bar at the bottom. On iPad, Meaning at the top opens the translation and the tafsir beside the page. It is a short explanation of what the ayah means, in 9 languages: Arabic, English, Bengali, Urdu, French, Spanish, Indonesian, Turkish and Russian. It is inside the app, so it works with no internet. Pick which one you read under View.

Which translations are there?

19, in 17 languages: English (three of them), Bengali, Urdu, French, Spanish, Indonesian, Turkish, Russian, Persian, Hindi, Tamil, Malay, German, Italian, Portuguese, Chinese and Swahili. Press and hold a word in the ayah view to see what it means. Word meanings are in 9 of those languages. Choose the translation and turn word meanings on under View.

The screen turned off while I was reading

It should not. While a mushaf page is open, the app keeps the screen awake so you can read to the end of the page without touching it. It goes back to your usual Auto-Lock as soon as you leave the page. In Low Power Mode, iOS can still dim the screen.

Where is my progress kept?

On your phone or iPad, in the app's own storage. It is not sent anywhere and there is no account attached to it. It is not shared between your phone and your iPad. Deleting the app deletes it, so if you are moving to a new device, use a device backup (on your computer or in iCloud) to carry the app and its data across.

The app is large

It is over 300 MB, because the mushaf is built from more than a thousand page fonts rather than pictures of pages, and three prints are included. That is what lets a page stay sharp at any size and match the print line for line. Install over Wi-Fi. Recitations are downloaded separately as you play them. To delete them, open Settings in the app, find Saved recitations under Audio and Salah, and tap Delete.

Is this a replacement for a teacher?

No. Hifz Quran helps you practise every day. It does not replace a teacher. Ask your teacher to check your hifz and your tajweed.

Still stuck?

Email contact@bluetomatostudio.com with your iPhone or iPad model and iOS version, and a short note about what happened. It helps us find the problem.
