What you need

  • An iPhone running iOS 17 or later.

  • An internet connection the first time you play a reciter; after that, audio is cached and works offline.
  • About 190 MB of space, and Wi-Fi for the first install.

Common questions

How do I repeat an ayah?

Open a surah and tap an ayah on the page. Tap a second ayah if you want a range. The strip at the bottom then says what you have selected: tap Repeat and it starts playing straight away. The sliders button beside it is where you change how many times each ayah repeats, how long a gap to leave for reciting along, and which reciter. With nothing selected, that same strip offers the whole surah.

How does testing yourself work?

Select the passage you have been working on and tap Test. The āyāt blank out on the page. Recite each one from memory, tap Reveal to check yourself, and at the end mark the run Solid or Shaky. That answer is what decides when the passage comes back around for review. Nothing listens to you. You check your own recall, the way you would with a teacher.

Is my recitation really private?

There is nothing to keep private, because nothing is recorded. Hifz Quran has no microphone access and no speech recognition. It asks for no permissions of any kind, has no accounts, no analytics, and no servers of ours. See the privacy policy for the full picture.

Do I need the internet?

Only for recitation audio. The mushaf, the Quran text, and all ten translations are inside the app and work offline from the start. The internet is used to fetch a reciter’s audio the first time you play it; after that it is cached on your iPhone. You can also download a surah ahead of time from the repeat settings.

What does it cost?

Hifz Quran is free, with no subscription, no in-app purchases, and no account to make.

Which reciters are included?

Ten: Alafasy, Al-Husary, Al-Minshawi, Abdul Basit, As-Sudais, Ash-Shuraim, Al-Ghamdi, Al-Muaiqly, Ad-Dosari, and Badr al-Turki. You can set per-āyah and whole-group repeat counts, recite-along pauses, and playback speed.

A reciter is greyed out

That will be Badr al-Turki. No ayah-by-ayah recording of his exists anywhere, so his āyah timings had to be derived from his surah recordings, and he is offered only on the 77 surahs whose timings were checked against the audio, all of Juz ‘Amma among them. On the rest, pick another reciter. The other nine have a separate file for every āyah and work throughout.

Where is my progress kept?

On your iPhone, in the app’s own storage. It is not sent anywhere and there is no account attached to it. Deleting the app deletes it, so if you are moving to a new phone, use an iPhone or iCloud device backup to carry the app and its data across.

The app is large

It is around 190 MB, because the mushaf ships as 604 page-exact fonts rather than pictures of pages. That is what lets a page stay sharp at any size and match the print line for line. Install over Wi-Fi. Recitation audio is downloaded separately as you play it, and you can clear it any time from Settings → Audio → Downloaded recitations → Clear.

Is this a replacement for a teacher?

No. Hifz Quran is a companion for your daily practice, a patient way to drill and review between lessons. The memorization of the Quran and the correctness of your tajwīd are best confirmed with a knowledgeable teacher.
